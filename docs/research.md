\#\# Space and NASA-linked work

\- \*\*AVOCADOS (Oct 2026):\*\* This study asks whether changes in spaceflight symptoms show up in speech. It measures speech rate, voice quality (harmonics-to-noise ratio, cepstral peak prominence, shimmer), pitch and pauses. Higher fatigue went with slower reading, about 4.4% fewer words per minute. Higher low mood went with about 7.4% fewer. Reduced voice quality was consistently linked to "brain fog" across three speech tasks. The authors present it as a low-burden way to monitor astronaut health on long missions. This is a conference poster, and the replication work is still listed as future.\[1\]  
\- \*\*USF Health Voice Center:\*\* Researchers there, including Yael Bensoussan, are evaluating voice biomarkers for space medicine.\[2\]  
\- \*\*Axiom Space "Voice in Space":\*\* It tests whether an AI algorithm can detect changes in voice pattern and listening ability. These changes are linked to zero gravity and to cognitive shifts. It uses electroglottograph measurements and scripted recordings such as reading, singing and pitch matching.\[3\]  
\- \*\*Apollo 11 analysis:\*\* Fundamental frequency and formant structure changed in space. The authors attribute this to physical and cognitive stress and to different air pressure.  The same work found that speaker-recognition systems struggle with astronaut voice changes in space.\[4\]\[5\]  
\- \*\*Fearless Steps:\*\* This project analyzes the Apollo-era NASA communication recordings with speech technology.\[6\]  
\- \*\*STS-41 shuttle experiment:\*\* It recorded astronaut speech to study the effect of microgravity. One astronaut's fundamental frequency changed much more than the other's.\[7\]  
\- \*\*Older NASA stress work:\*\* NASA technical reports include 1980s voice-stress analysis research.  Another report is a 1970s analysis of Skylab communications with a psychological stress evaluator.\[8\]\[9\]  
\- \*\*Related NASA monitoring, not voice:\*\* The Individualized Stress Detection System tracks heart rate, heart rate variability and sleep.  The Psychomotor Vigilance Test (PVT) Reaction Self Test checks fatigue effects on performance.  The NeuroSTAR study at Penn examines stressor-related impairment.\[10\]\[11\]\[12\]

\#\# Terrestrial research

\- \*\*NIH Bridge2AI-Voice:\*\* This project is building a large voice dataset covering neurological, voice, mood, respiratory and pediatric conditions.  A 2026 study used it to detect laryngeal lesions. It reported a cross-validated AUC of 0.812, sensitivity of 0.870 and specificity of 0.566.\[13\]\[14\]\[15\]  
\- \*\*Depression:\*\* One review found voice measures had accuracy of 78–96%. A study of about 15,000 adults using 25-second speech clips identified moderate-to-severe depression more than 70% of the time.  The APA says this is not yet ready for clinical practice.\[16\]  
\- \*\*Longitudinal mental health tracking:\*\* A composite vocal score tracked symptom changes in outpatient psychiatry. It held up under real-world noise.  Sonde's 104-person validation study found a similar correlation with symptom severity. It is company-run research.\[17\]\[18\]  
\- \*\*Heart failure:\*\* A 2026 review covers voice for screening and for predicting hospitalization and mortality.\[19\]  
\- \*\*COVID and respiratory illness:\*\* An 80-person study detected 69% of asymptomatic COVID-positive people. It was limited to Hebrew speakers.  Sonde and Qualcomm are building a platform that claims to detect respiratory symptoms from a 6-second sample.\[20\]\[21\]  
\- \*\*Other NIH-funded work:\*\* Teams are looking at anxiety, ALS, and depression in people with dementia.\[14\]

\#\# Caveats

No FDA-approved voice analytics for clinical use existed as of the 2025 review I found.  Researchers also flag problems with standardization, real-world performance, and differences across populations.\[16\]\[20\]
